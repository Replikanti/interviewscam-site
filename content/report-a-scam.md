Did a recruiter send you a meeting app, a link or a coding test that looked wrong? Send what you have. Your report can warn the next candidate who gets the same invite.

If you already installed or ran something, [protect your accounts first](/before-your-call/#already-ran) and report afterwards.

## What to send

- The name the scam used, for example a company, a recruiter or a meeting app.
- Where the contact started, for example a job board, LinkedIn, Telegram or email.
- The meeting link, the repository or the package you were asked to open or run.
- A few sentences on what happened, with dates. Say whether you installed or ran anything.

## Evidence that helps

- **Screenshots** of the messages and the invite. Black out your own name, email address, phone number and anything else about you before you send them.
- **Message headers** if the invite came by email. Most mail apps show them under an option like "Show original" or "View source".
- **URLs and domains** exactly as you received them, copied as text. Do not open them again to check.
- **File names and SHA-256 hashes** of anything you downloaded. Send the hash, never the file itself. To get the hash, run `sha256sum file` on Linux, `shasum -a 256 file` on macOS or `Get-FileHash file` in Windows PowerShell.
- **Repository and package names**, and the account that shared them.

Do not attach the installer, the test code or any other file you were asked to run.

## How you are protected

- Your name and contact details are never published. You stay anonymous to the public, but not to the moderator, who reads your email and may write back with questions.
- Your messages and evidence are not published. A published page shows the artifacts and a short summary of the evidence, never your raw messages or your personal data.
- Nothing is published before the moderator has reviewed the evidence. The [methodology](/methodology/) explains how.
- Sending a report is voluntary. Without evidence, nothing is published.
- You can ask for your report and your evidence to be deleted at any time. The [privacy page](/privacy/) lists who handles your email on its way to the moderator and how long evidence is kept.
